01

Tokens Are the Unit of Cost

You don't pay per word or character. You pay per token — a chunk of text that might be a whole word, part of a word, or a single character. The meter runs on both sides: tokens in and tokens out.

04

Cost at Scale

A prototype at $0.02 per request feels free. Multiply by real usage and it stops being free very fast. Three variables, each varying by orders of magnitude:

requests × tokens/req × $/token = cost

Key Takeaways

What Every PM Should Know

1
Tokens are the unit. Not words, not characters. Understand tokens and you can estimate costs before writing a line of code.
2
Output costs 3–5× more than input. Classification (long in, short out) is fundamentally cheaper than generation (short in, long out). Design accordingly.
3
Match the model to the task. A small model classifies support tickets just as well as a large one — at 1/60th the cost.
4
Small decisions compound at scale. Cutting a prompt from 1,000 to 500 tokens saves tens of thousands per month at production volume.
5
Use model routing. Route simple requests to small models, hard ones to large. This single pattern cuts costs 70–80%.
