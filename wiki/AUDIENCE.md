# Audience Definition

## One-liner

Builders shipping AI-powered products — regardless of whether they came up
through engineering, product, design, or founded their own thing.

## Who they are

People actively making **build decisions** about AI products. Past curiosity,
past "what is AI?" — they're asking "how do I ship this well?"

### Three flavors, one site

- **PM-builder** — Knows the user and the business problem. Now uses AI tools
  to build the solution themselves. Needs enough technical depth to build,
  debug, and make sharp decisions. The vibe coding series speaks to this person.

- **Engineer, new to AI** — Ships production software but hasn't worked with
  LLMs before. Doesn't need to learn what an API is — needs to learn the fifty
  judgment calls between API call and shipped product. Product judgment and
  AI-specific craft are the gaps.

- **Founder / indie hacker** — Wearing every hat, building fast, shipping solo
  or with a tiny team. Needs the conceptual model AND the craft AND the product
  thinking, all in one place.

### Adjacent readers (welcome but not primary)

Data scientists evaluating model quality, solutions architects choosing between
RAG and fine-tuning, technical writers documenting AI behavior. They find
specific articles useful but aren't coming for the full journey.

### Explicitly not the audience

Non-technical professionals who want to "understand AI" at a literacy level.
That's a different site — the generic AI explainer space, which is crowded.
The moment we write for that audience, the content gets watered down.

## What unites them

- Past curiosity — actively building or about to build
- Need the **decision layer** — not "what is RAG" but "should I use RAG here,
  and how do I know it's working"
- Ship under real constraints (cost, latency, reliability, user trust)
- Need enough theory to debug problems, product judgment to make tradeoffs,
  practical craft to ship well, and honest analysis instead of hype

## The boundary

**If your job involves deciding how an AI product gets built and shipped, this
site is for you. If you just want to know what AI is, you're not the audience.**

## What the site gives them that nothing else does

The **decision layer**. Every AI tutorial teaches you to build the thing.
Nobody teaches you to build the right thing, or to know when the thing you
built is good enough to ship. That's the gap.

Articles like "cost-quality-latency triangle," "the automation threshold," and
"compound failure in agent loops" are the ones people remember — they're
decision frameworks, not implementation guides.

## Content implications

- Keep conceptual foundations (everyone needs them) but frame for builders,
  not observers
- Add more "craft" articles — the how-to-ship-well pieces
- Widgets evolve from "see how this works" to "try configuring this yourself"
- Don't dumb down, don't academic-up — stay at "confident practitioner" level
- The test for every article: **would this help someone ship a better AI
  product next week?**

## Evolution note

The site started as "AI for PMs" (2024 framing). Evolved to "AI for Builders"
because in 2026 the PM/engineer/founder roles are converging around AI product
development. The existing 19 articles mostly hold — the shift is in positioning,
what comes next, and how we frame the front door.

Tagline energy: **Learn the concepts. Build the judgment. Ship the product.**
