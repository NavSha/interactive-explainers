# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Builders shipping AI-powered products — three flavors of the same reader:

- **PM-builders** who know the user and business problem and now build solutions themselves with AI tools.
- **Engineers new to AI** who ship production software but need the fifty judgment calls between API call and shipped product.
- **Founders / indie hackers** wearing every hat, needing concepts, craft, and product thinking in one place.

They read in focused sittings (long-form articles, 10–20 min), often linked from social or word of mouth. They are past "what is AI?" — they're asking "how do I ship this well?" Explicitly NOT the audience: non-technical readers seeking AI literacy.

## Product Purpose

"AI for Builders" (interactive-explainers) is a static site of interactive guides explaining AI concepts — 8 series, 30+ articles, each with hands-on widgets in the explorable-explanations tradition. Its unique value is the **decision layer**: not "what is RAG" but "should I use RAG here, and how do I know it's working." Success = a reader ships a better AI product next week because of an article. Tagline energy: *Learn the concepts. Build the judgment. Ship the product.*

## Brand Personality

Confident, conversational, craft-forward. Humor at 2.5–3/5: dry wit, rhetorical questions, bold one-liners, rich analogies — never clownish. Each article opens with an original 4-bar rap-verse epigraph (Ben Horowitz-inspired); that playfulness is a deliberate brand system, not decoration. The reading experience should feel like a sharp practitioner explaining things over coffee: warm, editorial, precise. Reference points: explorabl.es (interactivity as explanation), Stripe Press (editorial craft signaling "this content is valuable").

## Anti-references

- **Corporate docs sites** (GitBook / Docusaurus / ReadTheDocs): utilitarian sidebar-docs energy with no voice.
- **AI-hype landing pages**: gradient-heavy purple-glow "unlock the power of AI" SaaS marketing aesthetics.
- **Academic / textbook**: dense, dry, citation-heavy, intimidating — the opposite of confident-conversational.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't tell.** Every concept that can be interactive should be. Widgets are the argument, not illustrations beside it.
2. **Practice what we preach.** A site teaching AI product judgment must itself demonstrate good judgment — fast, accessible, no hype aesthetics.
3. **Editorial confidence.** Typography and pacing carry authority. The reader should feel they're holding something crafted, not generated.
4. **The decision layer wins.** When designing a widget or layout, favor "try configuring this yourself" over "watch how this works."
5. **One voice, eight accents.** Per-topic accent colors give each series identity; the shared serif/sans system keeps the site one coherent publication.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG AA**: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large text ≥3:1. Watch the muted secondary gray (#6B6B6B) on tinted surfaces.
- Every widget animation needs a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative.
- Interactive widgets must be keyboard-operable and make sense when read linearly.
- No information conveyed by color alone (per-topic accents are identity, not semantics).
